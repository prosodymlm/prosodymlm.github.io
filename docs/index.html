Abstract

Current multimodal systems can describe what happens in a video, but their commentary is correct and flat: a goal and a throw-in are read out in the same voice. We introduce streaming multimodal prosodic commentary generation, in which a model watches incoming video and environmental audio and continuously produces commentary whose word-level prosody — pitch, energy, duration, and pauses — tracks the importance of what it sees and hears.

To support this task, we build a dataset from sports broadcasts by separating commentary from environmental sound and aligning each word with its prosody, the video frames, and the ambient audio. We then propose ProsodyMLM, a streaming multimodal language model with a multi-route LoRA prosody decoder that predicts discrete prosody tokens alongside text. It is trained in two stages: first, text–prosody knowledge is distilled from ProsodyLM; then, the model is fine-tuned on multimodal data. This keeps the pretrained backbone's abilities while grounding expressiveness in visual events and crowd sound.

ProsodyMLM outperforms TTS, prosody-LM, and vision-conditioned baselines on prosody-token RMSE, F0 RMSE, and emotion similarity. In LLM-as-a-judge and user studies on World Cup footage, raters preferred it for overall quality and event–prosody alignment.

Method

A streaming multimodal encoder keeps every visual and environmental-audio token for the current segment. Older segments are mean-pooled, so the history stays compact. A trainable projector maps frozen audio-encoder features into the language model.

The prosody decoder represents each word as SIL, word, P1…P5: preceding silence, phoneme duration, log-F0 range, median, and slope, and log-energy, each quantized into 513 bins. Prosody tokens get their own embedding tables and a separate slot position encoding, which leaves the text RoPE unchanged. A deterministic router sends each attribute through its own LoRA and 513-way head, so silence, duration, pitch, and energy don't compete for the same gradients.

Related Links
